# Graphic Design for beginners

Welcome! This course teaches you to **make things that look good and read fast** — posters, logos, social media posts and web interfaces. You need no drawing talent and no expensive software: we work in **Figma** and **Canva**, both free and right in the browser.

## What you will build

- A **poster** for a school event (olympiad, show, project fair)
- A **logo** and the rules for using it
- A **colour palette** and a **font pairing** you can reuse anywhere
- A set of **social media posts** in square and story formats
- A **web page mockup** ready to hand over to a developer
- The **complete visual identity** of the computer science club — final project
- An **interactive prototype** of a landing page, with screens linked together

## Prerequisites

- A computer running Windows / macOS / Linux, or a tablet with a browser
- A modern browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge)
- An email address for the free Figma and Canva accounts
- No prior experience and no drawing skills required

## Estimated duration

- **~17 sessions** of 90 minutes
- Recommended pace: one lesson per week
- Period: September – June (one school year)

## Tools used

| Tool | What for | Where |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Figma** | Vector design, interfaces, prototypes | [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/) |
| **Canva** | Quick materials: posters, posts, presentations | [canva.com](https://www.canva.com/) |
| **Google Fonts** | Free fonts, usable in web projects too | [fonts.google.com](https://fonts.google.com/) |
| **Coolors** | Generating and testing colour palettes | [coolors.co](https://coolors.co/) |
| **Unsplash** | Free photos with no usage restrictions | [unsplash.com](https://unsplash.com/) |

!!! tip "Student accounts"
    Figma offers its **Education** plan free to students and teachers, and Canva has **Canva for Education** for school accounts. Both are obtained with a school email address. The course works fully on the regular free plans as well.

## How it connects to the other courses

Graphic design does not stop at the image. What you draw in Figma in lesson 14 can then be built in HTML and CSS — see the [Web Development course](../web/index.md), especially the lessons on [CSS introduction](../web/06-css-introducere.md) and [Flexbox and Grid](../web/08-css-flexbox-grid.md).

---

!!! tip "How to use this course"
    Do not copy ready-made templates. In every lesson make your own version, even if it looks clumsy at first — the eye trains through repetition. Keep all files in a single Figma project: at the end you will clearly see how far you have come.
